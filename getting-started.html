The fundamentals

Every cup of coffee is controlled by four dials: the beans, the water, the grind, and the ratio. Learn these and you can fix almost any brew.

1. Beans

Freshness matters more than price. Whole beans roasted within the last two to four weeks taste noticeably better, and grinding them just before brewing keeps the aromatic compounds that make coffee interesting.

Buy whole, grind fresh Pre-ground coffee goes stale within minutes of grinding. A cheap hand grinder and whole beans will beat expensive pre-ground coffee every time.

2. Water

Coffee is about 98% water, so the water quality sets a hard ceiling on your cup. Use filtered or soft tap water, never distilled (it lacks the minerals that help extract flavour).

Temperature matters too. Aim for around 90–96 °C (195–205 °F). If you don't have a thermometer, let boiling water sit for about 30 seconds before pouring.

3. Grind size

Grind size controls how fast water extracts flavour. Finer particles expose more surface area, so water extracts faster. The right setting depends on your brewing method:

Grind size by brewing method
MethodGrindFeel
French pressCoarseLike coarse sea salt
Pour overMediumLike table salt
AeroPressMedium-fineLike fine sand
Moka potFineLike granulated sugar

4. Coffee-to-water ratio

The ratio is written as coffee to water. A common starting point is 1:16 — one gram of coffee for every sixteen grams of water. That means 15 g of coffee to 240 ml of water.

Use a scale rather than a scoop; coffee density varies wildly. Prefer a lighter ratio like 1:15 for a stronger cup, or 1:17 for something lighter.

5. Your first brew

A simple checklist to get a repeatable, decent cup on your first try:

  1. Weigh 15 g of whole beans and grind them for your method.
  2. Heat 240 ml of filtered water to just off the boil.
  3. Brew following the recipe for your method.
  4. Let it cool slightly, taste, and note one thing you'd change.

When you're ready, pick a method and follow a full recipe.
