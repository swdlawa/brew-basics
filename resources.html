Resources & troubleshooting

When a cup goes wrong, there's usually a simple fix. Here are the most common problems, a glossary, and answers to the questions everyone asks first.

Troubleshooting

Common problems and their fixes

Bitter or harshOver-extractionGrind coarser, or shorten the brew
Sour or sharpUnder-extractionGrind finer, or lengthen the brew
Weak and wateryToo little coffee or too coarseIncrease the dose, or grind finer

Frequently asked questions

Do I really need a scale?
For consistent results, yes. Coffee is light and variable, so scoops are unreliable. A cheap 0.1 g scale is the highest-value purchase you can make.
Is a dark roast stronger?
Not in caffeine — darker roasts can actually have slightly less caffeine by weight. "Stronger" usually refers to taste. If you want more caffeine, use more coffee rather than a darker roast.
How long do beans stay fresh?
Flavour peaks from a few days to about a month after roasting. Store beans in an airtight container away from light and heat — never in the freezer you open often, and not in the fridge.
Can I just use tap water?
If your tap water tastes good on its own, it will usually make good coffee. Very hard or chlorinated water should be filtered first. Avoid distilled water, which can make coffee taste flat.
What's the easiest method for a beginner?
The French press. It needs no filters or technique beyond a coarse grind and a four-minute steep, and it's very forgiving of small mistakes.

Glossary

Bloom
The first small pour that wets the grounds and releases trapped carbon dioxide, helping even extraction.
Body
How heavy or thick the coffee feels in your mouth. French press is full-bodied; pour over is lighter.
Extraction
The process of dissolving flavour from the grounds into the water. Aim for balanced — not under or over.
Grind size
How finely the beans are ground. Finer grinds extract faster; coarser grinds extract slower.
Ratio
The amount of coffee relative to water, written as coffee:water. A common starting ratio is 1:16.
Over-extraction
Brewing too long or grinding too fine, pulling out bitter, harsh flavours.
Under-extraction
Brewing too briefly or grinding too coarse, leaving sour, thin flavours behind.
Single origin
Beans from one specific region or farm, prized for their distinct flavour profile.
