Brewing methods

Four popular ways to brew, each with its own character. Pick whichever matches the gear you own, and use the copy button to save a recipe.

Pour over

A clean, bright cup that highlights delicate flavours. You'll need a dripper, a paper filter, and a gooseneck kettle (though any kettle works to start).

Pour over — 1 cup Coffee: 15 g, medium grind Water: 240 ml at 93 °C 1. Rinse the filter with hot water, then discard the rinse water. 2. Add coffee, level the bed, and zero the scale. 3. Pour 45 ml and wait 30 s (the bloom). 4. Slowly add the rest in gentle circles, finishing by 2:30. 5. Swirl once; the brew should finish draining by 3:00.

French press

Full-bodied and forgiving — the easiest method to get right. It only needs the press itself and a coarse grind.

French press — 1 cup Coffee: 15 g, coarse grind Water: 240 ml at 93 °C 1. Add coffee to the empty press. 2. Pour all the water in one go. 3. Stir after 1 minute. 4. Place the lid (plunger up) and wait until 4:00. 5. Press slowly and serve immediately.

AeroPress

Fast, flexible, and nearly indestructible. Great for one strong cup and for travelling.

AeroPress — 1 cup Coffee: 14 g, medium-fine grind Water: 220 ml at 90 °C 1. Place a filter in the cap and rinse it. 2. Add coffee, then all the water, and stir. 3. Insert the plunger to stop the drip, wait until 2:00. 4. Swirl gently and press slowly for ~30 s. 5. Dilute with hot water to taste.

Moka pot

Stovetop espresso-style coffee — strong and concentrated. Treat it gently: heat low and slow to avoid a burnt, bitter cup.

Moka pot — 2 servings Coffee: 16 g, fine grind Water: fill to the safety valve (about 120 ml) 1. Fill the base with hot water up to the valve. 2. Fill the basket loosely with coffee, level, don't tamp. 3. Assemble and place on low heat. 4. Remove from heat as soon as it starts to sputter. 5. Stir before serving; dilute to taste.
